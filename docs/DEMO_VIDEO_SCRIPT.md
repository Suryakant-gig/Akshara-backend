# Akshara — Demonstration Video Script (target: ~2.5–3 minutes)

Record your screen while running the app locally (see README.md for setup).
Use a screen recorder with your voice narrating live, or record silent
screen capture and add narration after — either works with this script.

---

**[0:00–0:20] Hook + problem (talking head or voiceover over title slide)**
> "5 to 20% of school-age children show signs of dyslexia. In India, most
> go undiagnosed — not because we don't know how to screen for it, but
> because the one validated tool needs an expert to run it, and every AI
> alternative was built for English, not for scripts like Telugu. We built
> Akshara to fix that."

**[0:20–0:45] Show the app — setup screen**
- Open the app in a browser, screen-share it.
- Type a child alias, select Telugu, click Begin.
> "A teacher — no clinical training needed — starts a session in seconds."

**[0:45–1:15] Handwriting task**
- Show the Telugu passage displayed.
- Draw a sample on the canvas (a few real strokes; doesn't need to be
  perfect Telugu — the point is showing the capture flow).
> "The child copies the passage. Underneath, the system is reading stroke
> geometry — spacing, baseline consistency, glyph structure — looking for
> the specific error patterns Indic scripts actually produce, not ones
> borrowed from English handwriting analysis."

**[1:15–1:50] Read-aloud task**
- Click record, read the Telugu passage aloud (or a placeholder phrase if
  pronunciation isn't confident — narrate that this is what a native
  speaker's read-aloud would normally sound like).
- Stop recording.
> "The read-aloud is analyzed for fluency — pacing, pause patterns, how
> much of the recording is actually voiced speech."

**[1:50–2:10] The code-switch moment — the key differentiator**
- Open the manual transcript field, type something with an English word
  mixed in (e.g. "అమ్మ school కి వెళ్ళింది").
- Point at the code-switch detector output in the eventual report.
> "This is the part other tools miss. Indian children mix languages
> constantly — completely normal. Watch: the system catches the English
> word and excludes it from scoring, instead of treating bilingual speech
> as a red flag."

**[2:10–2:35] Numeracy + submit + report**
- Fill in the numeracy answers, click Finish & See Report.
- Show the report screen: risk badge, signal breakdown, code-switch note.
> "The teacher gets a plain-language flag — never a diagnosis, always a
> clear next step — with every signal that contributed shown transparently.
> No black box."

**[2:35–2:55] Close**
> "This is a working prototype, not a mockup — everything you just saw ran
> live, end to end. Full source code and documentation are in our
> submission. Akshara: built for the children global AI tools were never
> designed to read correctly."

---

## Notes for recording
- If your Telugu handwriting/pronunciation isn't confident, say so on
  camera briefly rather than faking fluency — the judges will trust the
  honesty, and it doesn't undercut the technical demo.
- Keep the report screen on-screen for a few extra seconds at the end —
  judges often pause the video there.
- If you have time for a second take, consider showing one run WITHOUT
  code-switching and one WITH it side by side, so the difference in the
  risk score is visually obvious.
